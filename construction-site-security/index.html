Material exit

Material loss often starts with an unrecorded movement.

Guards, cameras and a perimeter wall can still leave gaps when deliveries, temporary storage, subcontractor access and scrap exits are handled informally.

Footage may show that an item left the site. It does not show whether the movement was approved, who approved it, or what the team did afterwards.

Urban Eye sets out the gate, store and reporting process so the project team can check material movement against an agreed record.

Construction Material Control Plan

The plan covers the controls that apply to the site.

  • Visitor and subcontractor entry records
  • Delivery checks at the gate
  • Entry and exit records for tools, materials and equipment
  • Exit permits where the project requires them
  • Temporary alarm and camera coverage
  • Guard patrol instructions and escalation contacts
  • A daily record for the site team

The project manager agrees the rules, approval process and reporting format. Urban Eye applies the agreed process at the gate and controlled areas.

FAQs

Construction security questions.

Do Urban Eye guards manage material exit permits?

The site can require an approved exit permit before materials, tools, equipment or scrap leave. The project manager agrees the procedure and approval contacts. The guard records the exit against that process.

Can temporary alarms and cameras move as the site changes?

Yes. Construction sites change by phase. Urban Eye can review device positions, coverage and access points as the work moves from structure to fit-out, storage or handover. The final scope depends on the site layout and service plan.

How is subcontractor access managed?

The site process records authorised subcontractors, visitors and deliveries. The project manager sets the access rules. Urban Eye applies those rules at the gate and controlled areas.
