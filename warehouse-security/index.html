The Warehouse Security Gap

Stock loss is often found after the record has failed.

A warehouse can have cameras and guards but still lose control when loading bays, staff doors, returns, temporary storage and after-hours access are not properly recorded.

Footage can show an event after it happens. It does not confirm whether access was authorised, whether stock should have moved, or who acted on an alarm.

The Urban Eye Approach

Urban Eye maps the movement points that matter and sets out the controls, records and response roles around them.

Warehouse Security Framework

The framework covers the areas that need control on site.

  • Perimeter gates, fences and external doors
  • Loading bays, roller shutters and delivery routes
  • Staff, visitor and contractor access
  • Stockrooms, cage stores and restricted areas
  • Cameras, alarm devices and fault checks
  • Alert routing, verification and incident records

The assessment identifies where each control is needed and who owns the action after an alert or access exception.

Warehouse Workflow Audit

A structured review before equipment is recommended.

01

Review movement points

Urban Eye reviews vehicle gates, pedestrian entrances, loading bays, staff doors, returns areas, high-value stock and routes that receive limited supervision.

02

Check existing controls

The review checks cameras, access records, alarm coverage, lighting, guard instructions, key control and the condition of existing equipment.

03

04

Test and record

After implementation, the site tests critical doors, alarm events, camera coverage and alert routing. The team records issues and assigns ownership for follow-up.

Monitoring and Response

An alert needs a defined response.

An alarm or camera alert only helps when somebody receives it, checks the information and knows what to do next.

Urban Eye can define the alert route, verification process, escalation contacts and incident record for the warehouse. The exact response plan depends on the site's operating hours, staffing and agreed service scope.

FAQs

Warehouse security questions.

Warehouse security depends on the layout, stock value, staffing, operating hours and existing controls. These questions cover common planning decisions.

How do you secure loading bays and roller shutters?

The approach depends on the door type, operating hours, nearby routes and stock exposure. Urban Eye reviews whether the area needs camera coverage, door monitoring, motion detection, lighting, access rules or a combination of measures.

Can wireless alarms work in a large warehouse?

Wireless performance depends on the building, steel, concrete, pallet racking, stock levels and device position. Urban Eye confirms coverage during the site survey and installation test. Range extension may be included where the site requires it.

Can you reduce warehouse shrinkage?

The review identifies weak movement points and gaps in access, detection, recording and response. It helps the warehouse team investigate discrepancies and manage repeat risks.
